🎥 Cinematography

The art and science of capturing images — camera choice, lenses, lighting, framing, and movement that give a film its visual identity.

Camera Angles & Framing

📐 Shot Sizes

Every shot has a "size" — the amount of the subject and background in frame.

  • Extreme Wide Shot (EWS) — Subject is tiny. Establishes location. Great for openers.
  • Wide Shot (WS) — Full body in environment. Shows context.
  • Medium Shot (MS) — Waist up. Standard dialogue coverage.
  • Close-Up (CU) — Face. Emotional connection with the character.
  • Extreme Close-Up (ECU) — Eyes, lips, detail. Tension and intensity.

🎬 Camera Angles

  • Eye Level — Neutral, relatable. Most common.
  • Low Angle — Makes subject look powerful, dominant.
  • High Angle — Makes subject look small, vulnerable.
  • Dutch Angle (tilted) — Unease, chaos, disorientation.
  • Overhead / Top Down — God's-eye view. Detached or explanatory.
  • Over-the-Shoulder (OTS) — Conversation coverage.

Lenses & Depth

🔭 Focal Length

Wide (14–24mm)

  • Big field of view
  • Distorts faces if close
  • Great for landscapes & interiors

Normal (35–50mm)

  • Similar to human eye
  • Natural look
  • Most versatile

Telephoto (85mm+)

  • Flatters faces
  • Shallow depth of field
  • Great for close-ups & compression

🌫️ Depth of Field

The area that's in focus. Shallow DOF (blurry background) isolates the subject. Deep DOF (everything sharp) is used for establishing shots and ensemble scenes.

  • Lower f-stop (f/1.4, f/2) = shallower DOF
  • Higher f-stop (f/8, f/16) = deeper DOF
  • Longer lens = shallower DOF
  • Closer to subject = shallower DOF

Lighting Basics

💡 Three-Point Lighting

  • Key Light — Main light. Placed 45° from subject.
  • Fill Light — Softens shadows on the opposite side of key.
  • Back Light — Separates subject from background.

🎨 Lighting Styles

  • High-Key — Bright, even, low contrast. Comedies, sitcoms, commercials.
  • Low-Key — Dark, high contrast, hard shadows. Thrillers, noir, horror.
  • Natural Light — Window light, golden hour. Indie and documentary style.
  • Hard Light — Direct, sharp shadows. Dramatic, stylized.
  • Soft Light — Diffused, gentle. Flattering and romantic.

Dolly / PushCamera moves forward/back on trackBuilding intensity
Truck / TrackingCamera moves sidewaysFollowing action
Crane / JibCamera rises or fallsReveals and epics
HandheldNatural, shaky movementRealism, tension
Steadicam / GimbalSmooth floating motionImmersive follow shots
ZoomLens focal length changesRapid emphasis (use carefully!)

💡 Cinematographer's Golden Rule

Every shot should serve the story. A beautiful shot that doesn't move the story forward is just decoration. Ask "what does the audience need to feel here?" — then shoot for that feeling.
