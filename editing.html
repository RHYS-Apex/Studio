✂️ Editing

Editing is where the film is truly assembled. You shape rhythm, pacing, and emotion — one cut at a time.

Types of Cuts

🔪 Essential Cuts

  • Hard Cut — Instant switch between shots. The default.
  • Jump Cut — Same subject, time jump. Creates energy or urgency.
  • Match Cut — Cuts between shots that visually or conceptually match. Elegant transitions.
  • Cross Dissolve — Gradual blend. Passage of time, dreamy transitions.
  • Fade In/Out — To/from black. Classic scene breaks.
  • Whip Pan — Fast pan blurs into next shot. Energetic, modern.
  • J-Cut — Audio from next shot begins before the picture cuts.
  • L-Cut — Audio from the previous shot lingers into next picture.

Editing Workflow

🗂️ Step-by-Step

  • 1. Organize — Label clips, sort by scene and take
  • 2. Sync audio — Align external audio with camera audio
  • 3. Rough cut — Assemble scenes in order. Get the story down.
  • 4. Fine cut — Trim every shot. Tighten timing.
  • 5. Add music — Find the emotional rhythm
  • 6. Sound design — Foley, ambience, effects
  • 7. Color grade — Correction first, style second
  • 8. Final export — Match delivery specs

Key Editing Principles

⚡ The 180-Degree Rule

Keep the camera on one side of an imaginary line between two characters. Crossing it flips their positions on screen and confuses the audience.

👁️ The 30-Degree Rule

Between two shots of the same subject, change the camera angle by at least 30 degrees — or it looks like a jarring jump cut.

⏱️ The Kuleshov Effect

Audiences derive meaning from the juxtaposition of shots, not the shots themselves. Show a face, then a bowl of soup — the face reads as "hungry." Show the same face, then a coffin — it reads as "grieving."

🎯 Cut on Action or Emotion

The cleanest cuts happen mid-motion or right when a line lands emotionally. Don't cut on the exact start or end — let the eye anticipate the change.

💡 Editor's Golden Rule

"Cut when the eye needs new information." — Walter Murch. If you're wondering where to cut, watch the frame without audio. Your eyes will tell you when they've seen enough.
