🎯 The Three Phases of Filmmaking

1

Pre-Production

Everything BEFORE you press record: writing, planning, casting, location scouting, scheduling.

60% of a film's success is decided here

2

Production

The actual filming: setting up shots, directing actors, recording audio, capturing footage.

25% of the timeline

3

Post-Production

Editing, color grading, sound design, music, visual effects, final export.

15% of the timeline (but can extend)

📝 Phase 1: Pre-Production

📄

Step 1: Write the Script

Every great film starts with a great story. Even if you're making a 2-minute short, you need a script.

📚 Key Elements

  • Logline (one-sentence summary)
  • Three-act structure
  • Character arcs
  • Scene descriptions
  • Dialogue

🛠️ Tools

  • Final Draft (paid)
  • WriterDuet (free tier)
  • Arc Studio (free tier)
  • Google Docs (format manually)

💡 Pro Tips

  • Write what you can actually film
  • Keep short films to 1-3 locations
  • Read scripts of films you love
  • Get feedback before filming
🗺️

Step 2: Plan & Storyboard

Turn your script into a visual plan. Sketch shots, note camera angles, and map out each scene.

📋 Key Documents

  • Storyboard (sketches of shots)
  • Shot list (numbered shots)
  • Floor plan (where actors stand)
  • Mood board (visual style)

🛠️ Tools

  • Storyboarder (free)
  • Boords (paid)
  • Paper + pencil (classic)
  • Pinterest (mood boards)

💡 Pro Tips

  • Never show up without a shot list
  • Always have a plan B shot
  • Plan for editing — think in cuts
  • Sunlight shifts — plan around it
🎭

Step 3: Cast & Crew

Even small films need people. Find actors and key crew members you trust.

👥 Minimal Crew

  • Director (you)
  • Camera Operator
  • Sound Recordist
  • Assistant / Runner
  • Actors

🛠️ Where to Find People

  • Local drama schools
  • Facebook groups (film, acting)
  • Reddit r/Filmmakers
  • Friends & family
  • Mandatory (online casting)

💡 Pro Tips

  • Feed your crew — always
  • Do table reads before filming
  • Confirm availability in writing
  • Have backup actors for key roles
📍

Step 4: Locations & Permissions

Find places that look right AND are legally safe to film in.

📋 Checklist

  • Permission from owner
  • Power access
  • Noise level (planes, trains, traffic)
  • Natural light direction
  • Parking / access

⚠️ Legal Must-Haves

  • Written location release
  • Public liability insurance
  • Film permits (public areas)
  • Music/art release for anything visible

💡 Pro Tips

  • Scout at the same time of day
  • Take photos and notes
  • Test sound at the location
  • Always have a backup location

🎥 Phase 2: Production

🎬

The Film Shoot

On the day, follow your plan but stay flexible. Every shoot has surprises.

📋 Call Sheet Essentials

  • Date & weather forecast
  • Call time for everyone
  • Location address
  • Scenes shooting that day
  • Contact numbers

🎯 Golden Rules

  • Coverage first — get a wide, medium, close
  • Record room tone (30s of silence)
  • Slate each take (clap / say scene-take)
  • Back up footage daily
  • Feed everyone on time

🎭 Directing Actors

  • Say "action" and "cut" clearly
  • Give specific notes (not "be sadder")
  • Let actors discover moments
  • Do at least 3 takes per shot
  • Get "safety" takes of important shots
🎙️

Audio Matters (More Than You Think)

Bad sound makes a film feel amateur. Great sound makes a film feel professional.

🎤 Best Practices

  • Get the mic close to the subject
  • Use a boom pole when possible
  • Use a lav mic for dialogue
  • Record 30s of room tone
  • Use headphones always

⚙️ Settings

  • Record at 48 kHz / 24-bit
  • Set levels — peaks at -12 dB
  • Turn off auto-gain
  • Check for hiss & wind

💡 Pro Tips

  • Kill noises you can control (fridges, fans, phones)
  • Do a test recording before each scene
  • Back up audio separately from video

🎞️ Phase 3: Post-Production

✂️

Editing

Where the film truly comes together. You'll cut, cut, and cut again.

🛠️ Free Editing Software

  • DaVinci Resolve (free — best)
  • CapCut (simple, mobile)
  • iMovie (Mac)
  • Shotcut (Windows, Linux)

🎯 Editing Workflow

  • Import & organize footage
  • Rough cut (assembly edit)
  • Fine cut (trim timing)
  • Add music & sound effects
  • Color grade
  • Final export

💡 Pro Tips

  • Cut on motion or dialogue
  • Start rough — refine later
  • Watch your edit on mute sometimes
  • Get feedback from non-editors
  • Save versions (v1, v2, v3...)
🎨

Color Grading

Set the mood and consistency of your film's visuals. Fix mismatched shots and add cinematic style.

🎯 Two Stages

  • Color correction — make it accurate
  • Color grading — make it stylistic

🎨 Basic Adjustments

  • Exposure & contrast
  • White balance
  • Saturation
  • Lift, Gamma, Gain (LGG)
  • Curves

💡 Pro Tips

  • Match shots before styling
  • Don't overdo it
  • Use scopes, not just your eyes
  • Teal & orange is a cliché — vary it
🎵

Sound Design & Music

60% of what the audience feels comes from what they hear.

🎧 Elements

  • Dialogue (clean, mixed)
  • Ambience (room tone, birds, wind)
  • Foley (footsteps, clothes, props)
  • Sound effects (doors, cars, weather)
  • Music (score or licensed songs)

🎵 Free Music Sources

  • YouTube Audio Library
  • Pixabay Music
  • Free Music Archive
  • Bensound (credited)
  • Incompetech (Kevin MacLeod)

💡 Pro Tips

  • Dialogue always wins — mix around it
  • Use silence for impact
  • Music should serve the scene, not the ego
  • Watch your mix on phone speakers too
📤

Export & Distribution

Get your film out into the world. Upload, share, submit to festivals.

📦 Export Settings

  • H.264 or H.265 codec
  • 1920×1080 (or 4K)
  • 24 or 25 fps
  • Audio: AAC 320 kbps
  • Format: MP4

🌐 Where to Upload

  • YouTube (Vimeo for pros)
  • FilmFreeway (festivals)
  • Instagram Reels (clips)
  • TikTok (behind the scenes)

🎬 Film Festivals

  • Start with local festivals
  • FilmFreeway is the main hub
  • Deadlines matter — plan ahead
  • Have stills, poster, synopsis ready

🎒 Gear Guide

Category Budget ($0–200) Beginner ($200–800) Pro ($800+)
Camera Your phone Canon M50, Sony ZV-E10 Blackmagic Pocket 4K, Sony A7 IV
Lens Phone lens (built-in) Kit lens 18-55mm + 50mm f/1.8 Prime set 24mm, 50mm, 85mm
Audio Phone mic + wind muff Rode VideoMic Go, lav mic ($30) Zoom H6, Sennheiser shotgun, boom pole
Lighting Window light + reflector ($15) Neewer 2-light kit Aputure 120D, softbox, gels
Support Book + rubber band Cheap tripod ($50) Fluid head tripod, gimbal, slider
Storage Phone storage 64GB SD card + external HDD Multiple SD cards + SSD array

💡 The Golden Rule of Gear

The best camera is the one you have. Great films have been made on iPhones, DSLRs, and borrowed gear. Focus on story, lighting, and sound before upgrading gear. A $200 microphone will improve your film more than a $2,000 camera.
