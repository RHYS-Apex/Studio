📝 Scriptwriting

Every film starts as words on a page. Good writing is the foundation — structure, character, and dialogue working together.

Story Structure

🏛️ The Three-Act Structure

The backbone of almost every film ever made.

Act 1: Setup (25%)

  • Introduce protagonist & world
  • Establish the "normal"
  • Inciting incident shakes everything
  • Ends with a decision to act

Act 2: Confrontation (50%)

  • Rising obstacles & stakes
  • Midpoint flips the story
  • Protagonist at their lowest
  • "Dark night of the soul"

Act 3: Resolution (25%)

  • Final confrontation
  • Climax — biggest challenge
  • Resolution of story arc
  • New "normal" established

🐈 Save the Cat! Beat Sheet (15 Beats)

Blake Snyder's popular structure for feature films — breaks a movie into 15 essential story beats.

  • Opening Image — before picture of hero's world
  • Theme Stated — the lesson the hero will learn
  • Setup — introduce characters & world
  • Catalyst — the event that starts the story
  • Debate — hero hesitates before committing
  • Break into Two — hero chooses the journey
  • B Story — usually a love interest or mentor
  • Fun and Games — the "promise of the premise"
  • Midpoint — false victory or defeat
  • Bad Guys Close In — pressure increases
  • All Is Lost — darkest moment
  • Dark Night of the Soul — hero's reflection
  • Break into Three — hero finds the solution
  • Finale — hero applies the lesson
  • Final Image — after picture, showing change

Character Development

🎭 Character Essentials

  • Want vs. Need — What the character wants (external goal) vs. what they truly need (internal growth).
  • Flaw — Every great character is broken somewhere.
  • Arc — How they change from beginning to end.
  • Backstory — Don't dump it on the page, but know it deeply.
  • Voice — What they say and how they say it should be distinctive.
